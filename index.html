I am an interdisciplinary health communication researcher, working across communication, public health, and nursing.

As a nurse in a cancer center, community health centers, and a school, I spent years on the delivery end of health communication, and that work is the ground my research stands on. My research examines how health communication can better serve people whose health risks and communication needs are often overlooked by general approaches, and how audience targeting, message design, and language support can make it more responsive to their experiences and needs.

What I study

  • 01
    Should prevention messages differ by diagnosis?

    Diagnosis is often treated as a clinical component. I ask whether it is also an audience characteristic: whether living with something changes how you understand the risk in the first place, and then how you respond to a message about it. My dissertation tests this with asthma and vaping, looking at how people with asthma talk about vaping in their own communities and whether they respond to prevention videos differently than their peers do. My goal is to listen to patients with illness experiences and develop health messages that reflect patients’ lives, and through them, to improve their health.

  • 02
    Do prevention videos work, and which features matter?

    Prevention campaigns rely heavily on video, and I ask two questions about it. The first is whether these videos work at all, which I examine through meta-analysis of the existing evidence. The second is what in them works: campaigns are usually evaluated as wholes, so I break videos down into content and structural message features to find which ones are effective and, ultimately, a recipe for the most effective prevention video.

  • 03
    What helps patients navigate language barriers in healthcare?

    A language barrier can affect every step of care, from understanding a diagnosis to deciding whether to come back. I study what helps patients whose first language is not English navigate that barrier. This includes professional interpreters, as well as the translation apps and generative AI that patients and health systems now use. I look at what works, what falls short, and how the support patients receive affects their trust in the health system and whether they stay in care.
