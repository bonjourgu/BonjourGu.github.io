What I study

01

Should prevention messages differ by diagnosis?

Diagnosis is often treated as a clinical component. I ask whether it is also an audience characteristic: whether illness experiences change how people understand a health risk and how they respond to messages about it. My dissertation tests this with asthma and vaping. My goal is to listen to patients and develop health messages that reflect their lives, and through them, to improve their health.

02

Do prevention videos work, and which features matter?

Prevention campaigns rely heavily on video, and I ask two questions about it. The first is whether these videos work at all, which I examine through meta-analysis of the existing evidence. The second is what in them works: campaigns are usually evaluated as wholes, so I break videos down into content and structural message features to find which ones are effective and, ultimately, a recipe for the most effective prevention video.

03

What helps patients navigate language barriers in healthcare?

A language barrier can affect every step of care, from understanding a diagnosis to deciding whether to come back. I study what helps patients whose first language is not English navigate that barrier. This includes professional interpreters, as well as the translation apps and generative AI that patients and health systems now use. I look at what works, what falls short, and how the support patients receive affects their trust in the health system and whether they stay in care.
